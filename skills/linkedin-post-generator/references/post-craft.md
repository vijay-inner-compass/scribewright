# Post craft

Guidance for writing LinkedIn posts that get read past the first two lines and that serve the chosen objective.

## Hooks (the first two lines)

LinkedIn cuts off the preview after roughly two lines, so the hook has to stand alone. Pick a hook style that fits the angle, and vary styles across a batch.

| Style | Example pattern | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| Specific result | "We cut onboarding from 9 days to 2." | Proof, case studies |
| Contrarian claim | "Most status meetings are the problem, not the fix." | Thought leadership |
| Short story | "The client said no, and it was the best thing that happened." | Branding, engagement |
| Direct question | "What would you do with a week back?" | Engagement |
| Numbered promise | "Three things I wish I knew before hiring my first engineer." | Teaching, lists |
| Confession | "I got this wrong for five years." | Trust, branding |

Avoid openers that waste the slot: "I'm excited to share", "Happy Monday", "Here's a thought", "Let me tell you about". If the first line could sit above any post on any topic, rewrite it.

## Structure

- **Hook**: one to two lines.
- **Context**: one or two short lines that set the scene (who, what, when).
- **Body**: the point, the detail, or the steps. One idea only.
- **Takeaway**: one line that states the lesson in the user's voice.
- **CTA**: one line, optional, matching the user's choice.

Blank lines between blocks. Single-sentence paragraphs are fine and usually better than long ones.

## Line breaks and formatting

- LinkedIn does not render Markdown. Do not use `#`, `**bold**`, or `*italic*` in the post text. Use plain text, line breaks, and simple symbols such as numbers, arrows (→), or bullets (•).
- Use emojis sparingly. Zero to two per post, and only if the user's tone calls for it.
- Do not use all caps for emphasis more than once per post.

## Length

- Short text post: 300 to 700 characters. Good for a single sharp insight.
- Standard post: 700 to 1,300 characters. The default.
- Story or list post: up to about 2,000 characters. Make sure the payoff is worth the length.
- Hard ceiling: about 2,500 characters (LinkedIn's limit is 3,000).

## Calls to action

Match the CTA to the objective:

- Branding and thought leadership: a question that invites a view ("Where do you land on this?").
- Lead generation: a clear next step ("DM me 'audit' and I'll send the checklist").
- Hiring: "We're hiring for X. Details in the comments" or "Know someone who fits? Send them my way."
- Launch: a link or a date ("Live from Monday. Link in the first comment.").
- Engagement: a specific question that is easy to answer in one line.

Put external links in the first comment rather than the post body where possible, since LinkedIn tends to reduce reach for posts with outbound links. Mention this to the user if they want a link.

## Credibility

- Prefer one concrete detail (a number, a date, a named tool, a specific moment) over three vague adjectives.
- Attribute quotes and ideas to their source when the source is public.
- Never fabricate a result. If the notes lack a number, write the post without it or mark the gap `[add real number]`.

## Pre-delivery checklist

Run through this for each post before it goes into the file:

- [ ] The first line works alone as a hook.
- [ ] One idea, one lesson.
- [ ] Every fact traces back to the notes or is flagged `[verify]`.
- [ ] No Markdown syntax in the text.
- [ ] Length fits the chosen format.
- [ ] Matches the chosen tone and objective.
- [ ] CTA matches the objective and is one line.
- [ ] Hashtags, if any, are at the end and number five or fewer.
- [ ] Nothing sensitive from the notes is included without the user's OK.
